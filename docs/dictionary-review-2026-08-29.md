# Dictionary review, 2026-08-29

Not a plan and not a task list. This is a byproduct: while writing one usage
example per variant, the writers and their checkers flagged entries where the
**dictionary itself** looks wrong, as distinct from the example being wrong.
The examples for these shipped anyway, because each one does demonstrate the
word as the dictionary defines it. What is in question is the definition.

72 entries carry a doubt. 123 more were dropped outright and got no example.

Nothing here has been verified by a native speaker. Treat each line as a
question to put to one, not as a correction to apply.

## Entries whose country or sense looks wrong

### AR

- **nabo** (loser): Real Argentine slang, but it lands closer to "idiot/dork" than to "loser".
- **portado** (obedient): Solo funciona dentro de 'bien portado'; 'portado' suelto no se dice.
- **resentido** (oversensitive): "Resentido" is bitter/resentful; for merely touchy an Argentine would say "picado" or "sacado".

### AR,MX

- **reventar** (to-flip-out): Se entiende, pero 'reventar' se lee primero como estallar o estar agotado; para la rabia es más claro 'explotar' o, en AR, 'sacarse'.

### BR

- **bro** (buddy-brother): Real but generational: common among younger urban Brazilians and online. 'Mano' is the version that works with everyone.
- **cansado** (tired): Correct Portuguese, but it is the plain word for tired, not slang; the slangy option would be acabado or morto.
- **esnobe** (snob): Understood everywhere but it is a borrowing, not street slang; Brazilians more often say patricinha or metido.
- **festejar** (to-party): 'Festejar' es celebrar; para salir de fiesta el brasileño típico es 'sair pra balada'.
- **inteligente** (smart): Standard Portuguese, not slang; the street-smart sense the concept describes is esperto, not inteligente.
- **os gambé** (police): Regional São Paulo periphery slang, common in rap and funk rather than nationwide.
- **p da vida** (furious-fuming): Es la forma censurada por escrito de 'puto da vida'; hablando se dice la frase completa.
- **quebrada** (home): quebrada means one's neighborhood or hood (periphery slang), not a house; it works for 'home' only in the 'back to my block' sense, so the gloss is looser than the entry suggests.

### CL

- **encabritarse** (to-get-angry): Es español general más que chileno de la calle; en Chile suena más natural 'se choreó' o 'se enojó'.
- **enchuchado** (angry): Enchucharse for getting furious is real Chilean slang but sounds dated to younger speakers.
- **hecho pico** (tired): In Chile hecho pico more often means ruined or broken; exhausted is a stretch of the same phrase, and it is crude.
- **hijo de mil putas** (bastard-scoundrel): Used in Chile but not particularly Chilean; it is a pan-Hispanic insult.
- **la callampa** (chile-specific-insults): The dick sense is real, but day to day callampa mostly means terrible (ser callampa) or a mess (quedó a la callampa).
- **pavo** (coward): En Chile 'pavo' se lee más como tonto o ingenuo que como cobarde; para cobarde se usa más 'cagón' o 'pollo'.
- **pelado** (broke): Used, but Chile more often says "andar pato"; in Colombia/Ecuador "pelado" means kid instead.
- **picar** (to-leave): The living Chilean form is reflexive, 'picárselas'; bare 'picar' on its own does not carry the meaning.
- **tole tole** (brawl): Chilean and natural, but it means uproar or commotion; it does not specifically imply people throwing punches.

### CL,CO,AR

- **bastardo de mierda** (bastard-scoundrel): Same caveat as bastardo: it is understood, but it sounds translated rather than local.

### CL,CO,AR,PE,VE,MX

- **listo** (smart-clever): Solid for CO, VE, MX, PE. In CL and AR 'listo' is heard mostly as 'ready/done'; for clever they lean on 'vivo', 'pillo' or 'avispado'.
- **qué diablos** (what-the-hell): Understood everywhere and used in MX, CO, VE, but it carries a dubbed-TV flavor. In AR and CL the live version is 'qué carajo' or 'qué mierda'.

### CL,CO,AR,PE,VE,MX,BR

- **agua** (water): En Brasil se escribe 'água' y la frase sería 'Me passa um copo de água que tô com sede'.
- **bastardo** (bastard-scoundrel): Understood everywhere but reads like dubbed-movie Spanish; in real speech people reach for hijo de puta instead.
- **bebida** (drink-beverage): Ojo con Chile: ahí 'bebida' significa específicamente gaseosa. En portugués 'bebida' existe igual: 'Que bebida você vai pedir?'.
- **dormir** (to-sleep): Frase en español; en portugués sería 'Vou dormir, amanhã tenho que acordar cedo'.
- **fumar** (to-smoke): Frase en español; en portugués sería 'Vou fumar um cigarro e já volto'.

### CL,CO,AR,VE

- **gaseosa** (soda): En Chile no se dice 'gaseosa' sino 'bebida'; la frase está en voz argentina, donde sí es natural.

### CL,PE

- **cachar** (to-understand): El ejemplo es chileno, donde 'cachar' y 'cachai' son de uso diario. En Perú el sentido dominante de 'cachar' es sexual, así que ahí la frase suena rara o directamente grosera.
- **cachuelo** (side-hustle): Solidly Peruvian; in Chile the odd job is usually a pituto or a pololo, cachuelo is heard but less common.

### CL,VE

- **los pacos** (police): Solid for Chile. For Venezuela I am not confident pacos is the local word for police; VE tends toward otras formas.

### CO

- **engallado** (arrogant): In Colombia "engallado" more often means decked out or flashy (a car engallado); the cocky reading is secondary.
- **maluco** (lame): In Colombia maluco usually means unpleasant, off, or physically unwell (me siento maluco), not boring.
- **mamacita** (attractive): Used across the region rather than being distinctly Colombian, and most often as a street catcall.
- **mañoso** (sketchy): In Colombia "mañoso" also commonly means picky or fussy, so context carries the shady reading.
- **ni por el chiras** (no-way): Paisa-flavored; not something you'd hear on the coast or in every region of Colombia.

### CO,AR,PE,VE

- **trabado** (high): Solid in Colombia and Venezuela. In Argentina I'd expect "fumado" or "volado" instead.

### CO,MX

- **agalludo** (gutsy): In parts of Colombia and Mexico agalludo also reads as greedy or grabby, so the admiring sense depends on context.

### CO,PE,VE

- **vaina** (thingamajig): Perfect for CO and VE; in Peru the all-purpose filler is usually 'huevada' or plain 'cosa'.

### CO,VE

- **jambar** (to-eat): La forma que oigo más es 'jamar'; 'jambar' existe como variante hablada, sobre todo en la costa, pero no estoy seguro de que sea la corriente en Venezuela.
- **pendenciero** (troublemaker): Understood everywhere but bookish; the everyday word is "peleón" or "buscaproblemas".

### MX

- **con la greña parada** (scared): greña is solidly Mexican for messy hair, but the fixed fear idiom is more usually 'se me paró la greña' or 'con los pelos de punta'; 'con la greña parada' reads plausible rather than common.
- **el tamarindo** (police): Chilango and dated: it comes from the old tan uniforms of traffic cops, so younger speakers may not use it.
- **el tiradero** (dump): In Mexico tiradero usually means a mess (qué tiradero tienes en tu cuarto). For the landfill people often say basurero or tiradero de basura.
- **grifo** (high): Real but dated Mexican slang (from "grifa" for weed); the current word is "pacheco".
- **madreado** (tired): Madreado usually means beaten up; the wiped-out reading works only with a physical-effort context like this one.
- **mamerto** (loser): In Mexico it reads "dopey fool"; in Colombia the same word means a leftist, so it doesn't travel.
- **pendejo** (gullible): In Mexico pendejo reads first as dumbass; the naive or easily-tricked sense only lands with a setup like this one.
- **tronado** (broke): "Tronado" also means high or mentally gone in Mexico, so it needs the money context.

### MX,BR

- **beber** (to-drink): La frase va en español mexicano; en portugués brasileño sería 'Ele começou a beber cedo na festa'.

### MX,PE

- **chupe** (liquor): Solid in Mexico. In Peru 'chupe' first reads as the soup (chupe de camarones); the drinking sense is usually the verb 'chupar' ('nos fuimos a chupar').
- **ponerse bravo** (to-lose-temper): Natural en Perú y el Caribe; en México lo normal es 'no te encabrones' o 'no te enojes'.
- **refresco** (soda): Muy mexicano; en Perú se dice 'gaseosa', y 'refresco' allá suele ser una bebida de fruta como la chicha.

### MX,PE,US

- **crudo** (hungover-slang): Sólido en México y entre latinos en EE.UU.; en Perú se dice 'estoy con resaca' o 'con caña', no 'crudo'.

### MX,US

- **güevonear** (to-goof-off): In Mexico it's normally said and written "huevonear", and it leans toward lazing about rather than messing around.

### MX,VE

- **pelarse** (to-leave): Natural in Mexico; in Venezuela 'pelarse' usually means to slip up or to miss something, not to leave.

### PE

- **caracho** (exclamation): Real in Peru but sounds dated; younger speakers reach for miércoles or a stronger word first.
- **cobista** (brown-noser): Real Peruvian slang but sounds dated to younger speakers, who'd say "chupamedias" or "lameculos".
- **el combo** (crew): Used, but 'la collera' or 'los patas' is the more distinctly Peruvian way to name your group.
- **hacer la rabona** (skip-class): Understood in Peru, but the everyday Peruvian phrase is 'tirarse la pera'.
- **huachafo** (bad-quality): Huachafo is tacky or trying too hard to look classy, not simply low quality.
- **opa** (stupid): Viene del quechua y se oye sobre todo en la sierra sur; en Lima es raro, ahí se dice 'zonzo', 'tonto' o 'cojudo'.
- **tranquilo** (obedient): Es palabra común y neutra, no jerga: significa calmado, y solo por contexto se lee como portarse bien.

### US

- **la yarda** (home): in US Spanglish la yarda most often means the yard or backyard; the 'my place / home' reading is an extension and depends on context.
- **wachear** (to-watch): The form you hear most is wachar (wacha esto). Wachear exists but is the less common variant.

### VE

- **curdo** (drunk): Curda and curdo for drunk are strongest in Argentina and Uruguay; usable in Venezuela but not the first word a Venezuelan reaches for.
- **pajúo** (liar): En Venezuela 'pajúo' es más amplio: cae entre idiota y hablador de paja, y es bastante grosero; no es un sinónimo limpio de mentiroso.
- **pelando bolas** (broke): In Venezuela it also (and often) means being idle with nothing to do.
- **real** (money): Heard in Venezuela mostly in fixed negatives like no tengo ni un real; less natural as a general word for cash.
- **ricura** (attractive): Also plain endearment ("sweetie"), not necessarily about looks.
